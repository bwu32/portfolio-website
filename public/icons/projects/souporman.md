# Soup or Man (2026)

"Soup or Man" was my first time stepping into the role of Director with complete creative and artistic control. Inspired by the 2025 *Superman* film, I reimagined the Man of Steel as the Can of Steel: Clark Canh is a humble pho shop owner who must defend the "soup community" against the rage-baiting schemes of Beli Connoisseur Le Lu-Ther. 

## The Director’s Burden: Scheduling & Logistics

Being in charge meant balancing more than just the camera. I was responsible for the entire production pipeline:
- **Cast Management:** Coordinating complex filming schedules with multiple cast members and a videographer across various campus locations (from Startup Shell to Stamp Hill).
- **File Crisis Management:** This project was a trial by fire in data integrity. I dealt with corrupted SD cards, lost footage, and the nightmare of Google Drive compressing our high-fidelity files. Managing the sheer size of a **4K workflow** taught me the vital importance of proxy editing and robust backup systems.

## Technical Execution: The LOG Nightmare

We decided to shoot on a professional camera in **LOG** to maximize dynamic range, but it turned into a massive color-grading challenge. 

- **Color Correction:** When Premiere Pro’s native tools struggled with the LOG footage, I pivoted and used **Adobe Lightroom** to salvage the color grades, frame by frame where necessary, to achieve the cinematic look the parody demanded.
- **VFX & CGI:** This was my first foray into "heavy" visual effects. I taught myself how to execute:
    - **Heat Vision:** Crafting frame-aligned laser effects.
    - **Flight Sequences:** Utilizing green screen and object masking to make Clark Canh fly over the horizon.
    - **CGI Stunt Work:** Integrating digital enhancements into the fight scenes to make them feel "super."

## Story & Reception

The plot centered on a fabricated civil war within the soup community—dipping vs. pouring pho—orchestrated by Le Lu-Ther. Despite the "D1 ragebaiting," Soup or Man stands as a protector of good soup. 

The film was incredibly well-received, proving that even with a "67% guarantee" of looking epic, the combination of sharp comedy and high-effort VFX resonated with the audience. It stands as my most ambitious technical achievement in videography to date.
